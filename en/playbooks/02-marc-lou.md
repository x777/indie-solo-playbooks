## Who this is and the numbers he publishes

French solo founder. After five years of failures he took a job in 2021, saw Pieter Levels’ path, and started again: tiny startups + writing about them in public.

Public claims as of September 2026:

- 36 startups, ~$3M lifetime revenue, solo, ~85% margin, no investors
- 50% of projects made nothing; only ~11% made “decent” money
- Portfolio (bio numbers, they move): TrustMRR ~$44k/mo, DataFast ~$26k, Ship or Die ~$13k, CodeFast ~$6k, ShipFast ~$4k, IndiePage ~$1k
- ShipFast (2023): $6k in 48 hours after Product Hunt, $250k in 5 months, zero paid ads at the start
- Meta ads for ShipFast in 2024 cost ~$25k and returned ~$75k (Nico Jeannen ran the campaign)

Jack Friks says ShipFast is what pushed him into solo SaaS.

## Philosophy

- Think in weeks, not years. Tiny projects. Quit even faster than you start.
- Do not get emotionally attached: “everything goes to zero sooner or later.”
- A 5–10% hit rate is normal for a founder. Optimism is deliberately “stupid,” otherwise nobody survives 7 years of misses.
- People sit at $0 because of bad marketing, not competitors. 100 products can live in parallel.
- If an idea does not take off — you need more marketing, not more features.
- The same stack forever (his: Next.js + Tailwind + MongoDB + Stripe). Speed beats perfect code.
- Freelance / agency / one-off apps / viral tools first if you need money fast. SaaS is hard to earn.

## Launch system

### Base loop (his most repeated scheme)

1. A one-feature product.
2. A landing page with a Stripe link (not “Sign up” — pay).
3. Launch everywhere: Product Hunt, Hacker News, Reddit, X, LinkedIn, YouTube.
4. One week of maximum push on the MVP.

If it does not take off:

5. Rebrand: new name, logo, design, landing headline. Same product. Back to step 3.
6. A new market segment with the same pain. Repeat 3 and 5.
7. If it is still quiet — pause and build something else. Come back later if you want.

### Launch checklist across platforms

In 48 hours:

- a teaser on X and in the newsletter;
- 5 prepared answers (price, stack, roadmap);
- ask 15 beta users for feedback, not upvotes.

Launch day:

- Product Hunt at 12:01 AM PST;
- demo GIF/video on X and pin it;
- cross-post to relevant subreddits as a story, not an ad.

After 24 hours:

- a thread with numbers;
- a thank-you;
- a “offer ends today” banner if there is a deadline.

The first 4 hours on Product Hunt are the most important window. The product name should say what it is immediately (DataFast, not an abstract VC name).

### Launch videos

His signature move: funny clips with his face composited into movie scenes. People wait for that format. The slogan sells the outcome, not features: *Ship Fast*.

## Marketing and distribution

1. **Audience first.** Years of building in public before the hit. At the ShipFast launch more than half the traffic came from his X (~40k indie-hacker followers = the ideal buyer of a boilerplate).
2. **Free useful tools as a funnel.** LogoFast added +25% daily traffic to ShipFast. Ahrefs does the same with a free keyword tool. Better without mandatory signup.
3. **Neighbor products for the same audience.** Not one $1M product — a portfolio for the same people.
4. **Social proof with numbers.** “Launched a week and a half ago — $450 MRR” beats abstract benefits.
5. **Time-to-value, not a feature list.** On the ShipFast landing it is immediate: how many hours it saves on Stripe / DNS / a landing page.
6. **Black Friday without dumping the flagship.** Bundles at the price of the main product (“the second one is basically free”). $10k in 36 hours with no PPC.
7. **Ads are not the first channel.** He says he “sucks at ads”; Nico ran the ShipFast campaigns. Organic + platform launches are the base.

## Product and price

- Freemium is a bad idea for a solo founder. He says ~3% of free users convert. Free users do not become marketers and do not give backlinks.
- Alternatives:
  - limited free credits if the product costs time (API);
  - a trial of 7 days max + card + auto-subscribe;
  - a separate free tool people can share.
- If people “won’t pay until they try” — the landing copy is usually the problem, not the missing trial.
- Pay immediately. Commitment = a card, not an email.

## Typical mistakes he names

- Building in the dark for three years → burnout. Drop the ego, treat every startup as a small bet.
- Adding features instead of marketing.
- Holding an idea for years because “I believe in it.”
- Hoping free users will spread the product.
- Starting with heavy SaaS when you still have no money and no market trust.

## 30 days in the Marc Lou style

1. Pick one pain you solve for yourself. One feature.
2. Landing + Stripe in 2–3 days.
3. Launch week: PH, HN, Reddit, X, LinkedIn.
4. If quiet — new headline/name/landing and another week on the platforms.
5. If still quiet — another segment or a new tiny product. Do not add 10 features.
