## Philosophy

- **Your own pain → a product.** Nomad List — he was looking for a city. Remote OK — he was looking for remote work. Photo AI / Interior AI — he wanted photos and a room render.
- **Speed as an advantage.** In 2022 he wrote: idea → product in 6–12 hours. A simple stack beats the “right” architecture. 70% is enough to take money.
- **Pay from day one.** A free tier does not validate demand. If nobody pays — that is already the answer, not “need one more feature.”
- **Hands first, then the machine.** Early Avatar AI / Interior AI orders he ran himself at night. Automate when the cycle repeats and there is money.
- **Shotgun → sniper.** Many small launches with Stripe. Whatever holds money for months gets the focus. Do not feed the rest.
- **Zero employees is a feature.** Not a stage “until we grow” — a deliberate complexity cap.
- **The product should sell itself.** 5 September 2026: he does not want to pump UGC and ads to make the thing “work.” “If it's good enough it should be able to sell itself.”
- **Every day is launch day.** Not one PH a year — constant shipping into the feed.

## Launch system

### Base loop

1. A narrow pain you understand on yourself.
2. A landing with one promise and a Stripe button. Not “Sign up” — pay.
3. Fulfill the first orders by hand. Just understand what people actually buy.
4. A soft launch to your own X feed. Then Product Hunt / Show HN if there is something to show.
5. 1–2 weeks: payments and repeats appear, or the project goes on pause.
6. If it holds — a narrow machine for the narrowest bottleneck, not a “platform.”

### How he actually launched the hits

- **Nomad List:** he collected city data himself. Then a page per city — that is already SEO, not a blog.
- **Photo AI / Avatar AI:** the AI-avatar wave. A minimal slice + pay + delivery. He did not wait for the “right” pipeline.
- **Interior AI:** also manual processing first. In 2025–2026 he added new models (including a room walkthrough), but the core is the same: a narrow paid result.
- **fly.pieter.com:** assembled fast on AI code, did not build an “agent factory” before first payments.

The landing headline is concrete. Not “Enter the new dimension of CRMs,” but “a CRM that has AI and doesn't suck.” He has separately praised copywriters who write that way.

## Marketing and distribution

1. **Build in public is a channel, not a personal brand.** Revenue numbers, screenshots, what broke, what shipped. The X feed at once gives traffic, feedback, and social proof.
2. **SEO as an asset, not a blog.** Nomad List ranks for thousands of cities because each city is a separate data page. Programmatic pages for demand, not “10 tips for nomads.”
3. **One audience — neighbor products.** Remote OK is not “a new market” — it is the next step for the same people as Nomad List.
4. **Creators beat PR.** Press logos do not feed you. One video from a person with a niche audience lifted MRR from ~$12k to $40–50k and held it.
5. **Product Hunt and Show HN are a shot, not a strategy.** A factual title. First comment — what it is, why you built it, what you want to hear. Not ad copy.
6. **Organic instead of an ad budget.** In August 2026: he was not spending on ads/UGC. X pays him for living there. In September 2026 he repeated: the product should be able to sell itself.
7. **Do not build a factory before traffic.** In 2026 he was poking indie hackers with complex AI pipelines and zero users. Hours AI saved on code should go to distribution.

## Monetization

- A simple price you understand in 5 seconds. Subscription where there is repeat (databases, jobs, AI generations). One-time where the result is one-time.
- Do not build unit economics on falling GPU/API prices. Set margin so you survive if infra gets more expensive.
- Do not sell cheap software only to other indie hackers: that is the market AI eats first. Better: people with a live pain outside that circle.
- A portfolio is more stable than one hit. Some products can be finished and left as rent.

## Mistakes he names

- Building for months before the first payment.
- Doing freemium to “get people first.”
- Confusing likes on X with product-market fit.
- Assembling complex infra / an agent factory at zero traffic.
- Clinging to one project out of ego. He ran a 12 startups in 12 months challenge specifically to unlearn quitting.
- Selling only into the indie-hacker circle when the buyer can already clone it in an evening.
- Waiting for a big technical moat to hold the price. In 2026 he says it plainly: Big AI eats small apps. You need either your own audience, or a niche a chat cannot reach in one message.

## 30 days in Levels’ style

1. **Days 1–2.** One personal pain. One promise in one sentence. A price.
2. **Days 3–7.** Landing + Stripe + manual delivery. Post on X the same day the pay button exists. Do not wait for PH.
3. **Week 2.** 10–30 real attempts to sell: your own feed, narrow communities, DMs to people with this pain. Every order by hand.
4. **Week 3.** If they pay — close the most painful delivery step and make one page for a search query. If not — change the promise or the niche, do not add features.
5. **Week 4.** A public launch only if payments and before/after already exist. Otherwise pause and the next narrow slice, not “one more month of polish.”
