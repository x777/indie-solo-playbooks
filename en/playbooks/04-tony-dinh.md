## Philosophy

- **Software he uses every day.** Bio: “Creating software I love to use.” DevUtils — his own scripts. TypingMind — he hated the ChatGPT UI.
- **Do not wait for the product to spread itself.** The main turn (April 2026): with DevUtils he waited for a viral. It did not happen. You have to look for customers. For him that became X. For others — email, SEO, cold outreach, ads.
- **Years of quiet ships + a platform wave = “overnight success.”** TypingMind was a weekend. The audience he had been collecting since 2021.
- **Some products can be finished.** DevUtils feeds him with almost no work. Not everything has to grow forever.
- **Do not sit on someone else's platform.** Black Magic died with the Twitter API. After that he went to a product where the client brings their own key (TypingMind).
- **He admits he is weak at ads.** October 2025: “I really suck at marketing.” The core is product + WOM + the X feed. He tries ads and does not expect a miracle.
- **Global English-speaking market + low costs.** Living in Vietnam / Bali and selling in dollars — more attempts on the same cushion.

## Launch system

### TypingMind — his reference

1. 1 March 2023 OpenAI opened the ChatGPT API.
2. Same day — a domain. That weekend — a working client on your own key.
3. 6 March — a tweet to people already following. Not “a month-long campaign.”
4. First sale → payment the same day. Start $9.
5. Every next day: feature → tweet → sales. He raised the price to $39 as soon as it was clear people would pay.
6. Product Hunt a week later, when there were already sales from X. Not the other way around.

### Repeatable cycle

1. Own pain, not “a market for indie hackers.”
2. A narrow slice in days, not a quarter.
3. Show it to people who already read you. If there is no feed, find people by hand — do not wait for luck.
4. Demand — raise the price. No demand — next tool.
5. If the product sits on someone else's API/platform — have an exit plan (sell or rebuild).

## Marketing and distribution

1. **X as the only working channel at the start.** The first $22k of TypingMind came almost entirely from the audience. Tweet formula: “hated the UI — built a better one over the weekend. Here it is.”
2. **WOM after the first wave.** In 2025 he writes it plainly: most sales are word of mouth. He believes a strong product + a first base pull the next people in.
3. **Product Hunt is an add-on, not the engine.** It worked for TypingMind because the audience was already there. For the later Pulse Revenue in June 2026, PH brought $349.
4. **YouTube guides.** The @TypingMind channel — tutorials, not a founder persona. A second-channel attempt for people searching “how to connect Claude / GPT.”
5. **New-model integrations.** TypingMind feeds on plugging in the next LLM on release day. Marketing through the product, not through content.
6. **Ads do not close for him.** LinkedIn Ads ~$2k → $0 (2024): traffic from Android/mobile, while people pay from macOS. October 2025: $600 → 2 conversions. His takeaway: ads work on a mass consumer product, not on a narrow pro-LLM tool.

## Monetization

- **Start with a license.** $9, then $39 / $59 / $99 lifetime on TypingMind. A low floor speeds up first money; price goes up when there are features and demand.
- **Then recurring B2B.** By fall 2025 the Team version was more than 50% of the month. That is how he left one-off purchases behind.
- **The client brings their own API key.** Higher margin, no token bill at the start, no “OpenAI raised prices — the business died” in the form wrapper apps with a shared balance have.
- **A portfolio, not one hit.** DevUtils stays as rent. New things (the game, Pulse, makeawebsite) he launches without dropping TypingMind.

## Mistakes he names

- Waiting for a good product to find people.
- Quitting with no cushion. He had 2 years of savings and a return to Vietnam as plan B.
- Building a business on an API someone can turn off. Black Magic proved it.
- Pouring ads at a “business audience” when other people on other devices pay.
- Clinging to one dying product out of pride. He sold Black Magic and built TypingMind.
- Confusing ad clicks with customers. 337 LinkedIn clicks did not become sales.

## 30 days in Tony Dinh's style

1. **Week 1.** A tool for a daily pain. If there is an API/platform wave — ship this week, not “when I finish it.”
2. **Days 8–10.** An honest tweet: what you hated → what you built → a link. Payment the same day the first person says they will take it.
3. **Week 2.** One feature and one post a day. If they pay — raise the price. Do not wait for PH to “start marketing.”
4. **Week 3.** Manually find 20–50 more people with the same pain. Do not turn ads on until you know the ICP and the device they pay from.
5. **Week 4.** Repeats — think about a team plan or a neighboring product. No repeats — do not polish “one more month”; next narrow tool.
