## Philosophy

- Software he uses every day. Bio: “Creating software I love to use.”
- Do not wait for the product to spread itself. April 2026: with DevUtils he waited for a viral. It did not happen. You have to look for customers. For him that became X.
- Years of quiet ships + a platform wave = overnight success. TypingMind was a weekend. The audience started in 2021.
- Some products can be finished. DevUtils feeds him with almost no work.
- Do not sit on someone else's platform. Black Magic died with the Twitter API. Then he went to a product where the client brings their own key.
- He admits he is weak at ads. October 2025: “I really suck at marketing.” Core is product + WOM + the X feed.
- Global English-speaking market + low costs.

## Launch system

### TypingMind — his reference

1. 1 March 2023 OpenAI opened the ChatGPT API.
2. Same day — a domain. That weekend — a working client on your own key.
3. 6 March — a tweet to people already following.
4. First sale → payment the same day. Start $9.
5. Every next day: feature → tweet → sales. Price to $39 once people paid.
6. Product Hunt a week later, after X sales. Not the other way around.

### Repeatable cycle

1. Own pain, not “a market for indie hackers.”
2. A narrow slice in days, not a quarter.
3. Show it to people who already read you. If there is no feed, find people by hand.
4. Demand — raise the price. No demand — next tool.
5. If the product sits on someone else's API, have an exit plan.

## Marketing and distribution

1. X as the only working channel at the start. First $22k of TypingMind almost entirely from the audience.
2. WOM after the first wave. In 2025 most sales are word of mouth.
3. Product Hunt is an add-on. Pulse Revenue in June 2026: PH brought $349.
4. YouTube guides on @TypingMind — tutorials, not a founder persona.
5. New-model integrations on launch day. Marketing through the product.
6. Ads do not close for him. LinkedIn Ads ~$2k → $0 (2024). October 2025: $600 → 2 conversions.

## Mistakes he names

- Waiting for a good product to find people.
- Quitting with no cushion.
- Building on an API someone can turn off.
- Pouring ads at a “business audience” when other people on other devices pay.
- Clinging to a dying product out of pride.
- Confusing ad clicks with customers.

---

## 30 days in Tony Dinh's style

1. Week 1. A tool for a daily pain. If there is an API/platform wave — ship this week.
2. Days 8–10. An honest tweet: hated → built → link. Payment the day the first person says they will take it.
3. Week 2. One feature and one post a day. If they pay — raise the price.
4. Week 3. Manually find 20–50 more people with the same pain. No ads until you know the ICP and the device they pay from.
5. Week 4. Repeats — team plan or a neighboring product. No repeats — next narrow tool.
