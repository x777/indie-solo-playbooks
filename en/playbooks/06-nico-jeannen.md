## Philosophy

- Ads work if they are done well. Not magic and not a mafia tax. Not for every product or every budget.
- 80% of the work is creatives and wording, not the ads-manager setup.
- Customers talk about themselves better than you or AI. Calls and interviews first, ads second.
- Do not quit on an early plateau. He almost dropped Talknotes — a year later he sold it for $200k.
- The dropshipping habit “did not take off in a week — next one” is fine when you have no money, and harmful long-term.
- Quality beats hacks: one strong YouTube video at 100k views because he spent a week on the details.

## When ads are a bad idea

- Little money and an unclear audience. You will burn budget testing angles.
- No runway for subscription payback (often $40 CAC at $12/mo).
- No grasp of the customer’s words. Generic ads “for everyone” almost never work.
- Unofficial “agents/automations” for Meta/YouTube Ads — a fast ban. Use the official Ads Manager.

If there is no budget — organic, interviews, product first. Ads accelerate a working offer. They do not replace the offer.

## Paid growth system

### 5 rules from his main thread

1. Beginners with no runway — forget ads.
2. Creative = 80%. Look for “relatable nuggets.” Scribvet example: users finished paperwork at night → the ad “No more late night catching up on paperwork” became the top creative.
3. A subscription is almost always unprofitable at the start. Count payback in months. An annual plan with a large discount covers monthly CAC.
4. Do not make “pretty ads.” Make relevant ones. Ugly text on a white background grew Scribvet.
5. The more competitive the niche, the heavier the creative. Early Talknotes needed a simple image. When competitors arrived, he had to hire creators.

### B2B from zero, ads only (his write-up to $1k MRR)

1. Interview someone in the industry. Without that he “could not have done anything.”
2. A sufficient MVP from the interview insights.
3. That person uses it and says “I love it.”
4. Landing and ads in the audience’s words. Simple image ads, no video.
5. No purchase data → Facebook optimization on signups.
6. Cheap signups do not activate → onboarding tests until people start using it.
7. They use it but do not pay → a trial inside onboarding.
8. High churn → demo calls, feedback, every requested feature until active-user satisfaction is ~90%+.
9. MRR crawls. Some weeks −$100, others +$150. No hack: offer + traffic + improvement.
10. Every dollar back into ads if the cash is not needed to live.

Honest downside of this case (he writes it himself): B2B with long sales, calls, a non-tech audience, and competitors who walk trade floors. Hardcore mode.

## What worked for him and what did not

Worked:

- not quitting the project after an early plateau;
- Meta ads as the main channel;
- quality (YouTube, free-app algorithms);
- value before payment: a 1-minute Talknotes demo with no signup;
- building what people clearly ask for (a Meta Ads course → $100k+);
- 1–2 month sprints with a concrete goal;
- tiny free apps when there are no ideas — they show which problems are worth monetizing;
- sport / a pause so he does not burn out.

Stopped working / does not work for him:

- SEO (courses, hours of content → almost zero sales; traffic from free tools did not convert);
- Twitter + Product Hunt as the main launch. PH for him is now just a directory. The X algorithm is also unreliable as the only channel.

## Product and LTV

- Raise LTV with a neighbor product for the same audience: they bought A for pain A — some will buy B for pain B.
- An annual plan is a unit-economics tool, not just a “discount.”
- Onboarding matters more than the creative if people arrive and leave immediately.
- Do not build a product you cannot use yourself if you hate sales and calls — even at $1k MRR that is hardcore mode.

---

## 30 days in Nico’s style

1. 5–10 customer conversations. Write down their phrases.
2. One simple image ad and a landing on those phrases.
3. If there is no budget — do not turn ads on. Offer and activation first.
4. If there is budget — a small test, optimize for the nearest event (signup, if purchases are rare).
5. Fix onboarding every day, not creative for creative’s sake.
6. Count payback. Without an annual plan a monthly subscription often does not work.
