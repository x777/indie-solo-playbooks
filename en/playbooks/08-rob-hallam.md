## Philosophy

- Validation is payment, not likes and not “I like the idea.”
- Sell by hand first (high-ticket MVP / agency), then pack it into software for the same audience. Liftoff is that layer.
- A public progress bar. Not “building a SaaS,” but “day 453, $30k of $100k.”
- The mid-stretch is where everyone leaves. The first $10k is easier than the middle of the road to $100k.
- A product for X sells on X. ~99% of SuperX traffic is his own tweets, not ads.
- There is no secret algorithm hack. There is a weekly content cycle.
- Copy a proven idea and make one part 10x better. Do not invent the wheel.

## Launch and validation system

Three methods he unpacked in the “From $0 to $22K” talk:

1. **Copy a working idea and make one part stronger.** SuperX is an X scheduler/growth tool, but with analytics of viral concepts.
2. **Sell a service first.** A high-ticket MVP for live money, then a product. Liftoff: $0 → ~$38k in a few months through clients, not a landing-page viral.
3. **Put a pay button on what already exists.** Do not wait for the “real” product.

Launch pattern:

1. X profile: a face, one sentence in the bio, a pin with the goal (“day N to $100k MRR”).
2. A daily video/diary. He had a 133-day streak. Now — weekly progress videos.
3. The product lives where the ICP lives. SuperX grows SuperX.
4. A pay button from day one. Then SEO (in September 2026 he separately wrote that he is resurrecting SuperX SEO).

## Marketing: the content cycle

His signature weekly cycle (newsletter *How to actually grow on X*):

1. **Entertaining.** Algorithm attention: a dance, vulnerability, a feature demo in motion. People stop the scroll — X shows them the next posts.
2. **Educational.** A thread, a teardown, a tutorial. People who arrived on the dance now learn.
3. **Inspirational.** A number and a story: “$30k MRR, day 453.” The path, not gloss.
4. **Convincing.** A soft product link at the end of a thread or after a milestone. If the three steps before it worked, this is not an ad.

Then back to step one. The same cycle on LinkedIn and Threads.

Profile before the first post:

- a face photo, a smile, do not change it often;
- one sentence in the bio;
- a pin with a measurable goal.

More practice:

- 20–30 people you talk to regularly. Live time on the platform is what the algorithm rewards.
- Video beats text if the goal is attention.
- Data on your own posts: what hit — repeat the format, not the topic.

## Mistakes that follow from his history

- Building for two and a half years with no payment and calling that validation.
- Posting only sales or only memes. You need the four-type cycle.
- Changing the avatar and bio every week.
- Waiting for a “real idea” instead of taking a proven one and making a piece better.
- Quitting in the middle when the progress bar stops moving fast.
- Pouring ads where the customers are not, instead of writing where they already sit.

## 30 days in Rob Hallam’s style

1. Profile: a face, a goal in the pin, one sentence in the bio.
2. If there is no product — sell 3 high-ticket MVPs by hand to the same audience.
3. If there is one — a pay button and an MRR diary.
4. Every week one full cycle: entertain → teach → show a number → sell softly.
5. 20 people from the niche — live replies, not like-farming.
6. One channel. For him that is X, because that is where the SuperX customer lives.
