## Philosophy

- Intensity wins. Wave + familiar UI + pay without a call.
- PLG. No sales team through $10M ARR.
- A familiar UI lowers friction. The viral tweet used a streaming chat like ChatGPT, not a “new paradigm.”
- *The biggest mistake bootstrapped founders make is acting like a bootstrapped founder.* At $8M you cannot behave like a side project.
- 80% of a founder’s work is decisions. The first decision took 30 minutes: ship the demo.
- Bootstrap ≠ lifestyle ≠ small goals. You can want $100M and still skip VC.
- $1M MRR is not “$12M ARR.” It is $1M every month.

## Launch system

1. **The wave.** The OpenAI API had just gone public. The use case was obvious; almost no clean product existed yet.
2. **Two-week MVP**, not a two-month “real platform.” Upload a PDF — ask questions.
3. **One demo on X.** His third tweet ever. Pricing was not even on the page yet.
4. **Tag the stack.** Langchain and Pinecone reposted — that amplified the viral.
5. **Pay immediately**, no sales call. Otherwise the viral dies in a demo queue.
6. The product later grew from “chat with a PDF” into a harness for customer-facing agents (chat, email, voice, WhatsApp, Slack).

## Marketing

His list after $4M ARR:

**Worked:** organic, influencers, paid ads, SEO, timing.  
**Did not work (then):** short form, newsletters.

By channel:

1. **X.** A demo, not “I launched a startup.” Viral does not scale: once clones appeared, influencers stopped posting the same thing.
2. **Reddit.** Not “here is my tool.” A story. In book roundups he made a free chatbot for a specific book — people opened the domain, which later helped SEO authority.
3. **Product Hunt.**
4. **X / LinkedIn influencers.** Their demo, not a “20% off” code. In 2023 revenue spikes followed those posts.
5. **SEO + ads** — once the viral cools. Viral gives the first wave, not the company.
6. **Self-serve.** If the demo requires a call, it is not PLG and the viral burns off.

## Mistakes

- Waiting for an audience. He had 16 people.
- Building an unfamiliar interface instead of a familiar one.
- Living on viral alone. After the first wave you need SEO, ads, and retention.
- Acting like a “small bootstrap” when the product already pulls $8–10M.
- Underestimating hiring. He named this as the reason he missed $1M MRR by the end of 2024.
- Taking VC only because “that is what AI companies do.”

---

## 30 days in Yasser’s style

1. Find a wave where the use case is already obvious and a clean product is still rare.
2. Ship an MVP on a familiar interface. One demo — one flow.
3. Post the demo and tag the stack. Accept payment the same day, no call.
4. Reddit — a story or a useful artifact for the community, not a landing-page link.
5. If the wave produced first paying users — turn on SEO and retention immediately. Do not wait for a second viral.
