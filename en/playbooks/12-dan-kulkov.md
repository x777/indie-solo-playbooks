## Philosophy

- The goal is paying customers by the simplest path. Play the easy level first.
- One audience. Even failed launches stack authority, content, and a list.
- Do not impress indie hackers. Do boring marketing where buyers live.
- Iteration beats a heroic grind. Cap at 40–50 hours.
- Patience: SEO, email, and an X audience grow slowly.
- Knowing your “no” list matters more than a list of “yes.”

## 10 growth lessons ($100k thread)

1. **Keep one audience.** 16 launches, 6 alive, 3 hits. Failures still work for the brand.
2. **Fix the funnel.** Living from Product Hunt to Product Hunt is a trap. Anyone who did not buy the first time should land on email. Formula: X emails this week → Y revenue next week.
3. **Test channels.** X, Reddit, Indie Hackers, PH, Google Ads, SEO, newsletter sponsorships, directories, cross-promo, HN. Almost nothing works on the first try. That is the only way to find a channel that matches your skills.
4. **Give more than you monetize.** Top-of-mind reputation first, money second.
5. **Build simple.** Hits were assembled in a week, with no technical moat. They work because they solve an annoying pain, cost a one-time fee, and have distribution.
6. **Charge more through value.** Not just raise the price: prompt updates +$20, a bundle +$50, unlimited strategies +$70. Same conversion effort, 1.5–2× more profit.
7. **A harsh beta.** Ask people out loud to destroy the product. Ego aside → fixes → launch.
8. **Find what is not yours.** His “no” list: coaching, communities, agencies, daily newsletters, cold email, Facebook ads, exit-intent popups.
9. **Cap the hours.** The first business died on 80-hour weeks: no time to think → bad decisions → even more work.
10. **Do not rush the marathon.** A year earlier he expected $15k/mo “in January.” It did not happen. That is fine.

## “How to get unstuck” protocol

His later operating checklist:

1. Kill the “vitamin” SaaS with three customers.
2. Talk to 30 people about their problems. Not indie hackers.
3. Build a very simple solution. Not necessarily SaaS.
4. Honest, unpleasant feedback.
5. A better version.
6. Fix the design: ugly does not sell.
7. A price that makes sense. Often a one-time payment, not a subscription “because MRR looks pretty.”
8. A calm launch: PH, Reddit, X.
9. Market until the offer is confirmed.
10. B2B — cold email. B2C — short video or ads if there is budget.
11. Do not touch SEO until the offer is validated.
12. Once daily traffic is stable — fix activation and price.
13. After 2–3 months: drop it or double down.

Avoid:

- an “indie hackers” audience;
- yet another productivity tool;
- a subscription for the sake of a subscription;
- accountability groups and networking instead of customers;
- a new idea in two weeks;
- a product for people you do not understand.

## What he says in 2026

- ASO still works for new apps. His apps are under 8 months old.
- “Just do ASO” is a recurring thesis.
- A week of marketing = zero new features. A feature nobody asked for does not double revenue.
- Market outside X. Stop impressing indie hackers.
- Bold experiments: button color does not matter; a lifetime plan can be a large win.
- Hard paywall: lifetime + monthly with a trial.
- Build a painkiller for people who have money and a repeating pain. Not subscription trackers for people who are broke themselves.
- It is fine to pause an app and ship a new one.
- Do not sleep on YouTube.

## 30 days in Dan Kulkov’s style

1. Pick one audience that is not indie hackers.
2. 30 conversations. If there is no pain — do not code.
3. A simple solution in a week. One-time price if you can.
4. Beta: ask them to destroy the product in words.
5. A calm launch + an email funnel, not hope in a single PH.
6. Two weeks of marketing only, no features.
7. Day 30: repeat purchases/traffic exist — double down. If not — pause and ship the next offer to the same audience.
